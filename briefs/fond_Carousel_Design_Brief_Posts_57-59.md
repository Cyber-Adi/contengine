## SECTION 1: BRAND SYSTEM

### Color Palette

| Role | Name | Hex |
|------|------|-----|
| Dark background | Slate Black | `#0A0A0A` |
| Light background | Off-White | `#F5F1E8` |
| Primary text (on dark) | Off-White | `#F5F1E8` |
| Primary text (on light) | Slate Black | `#0A0A0A` |
| Highlight / CTA / key words | Steel Blue | `#4A7FAC` |
| Waste / loss / negative data | Signal Red | `#D93A2B` |
| Emotional accent / warmth | Warm Gold | `#D4A843` |

**Rules:**
- Signal Red is used ONLY for waste, loss, or negative data. Never decoratively.
- Warm Gold is used for emotional beats, key reframe words, and the "rescue / saved" element.
- Steel Blue is the app's voice — used on CTAs, reframe words, and featured terms.
- Never use gradient backgrounds or drop shadows.

### Typography

| Role | Font | Weight |
|------|------|--------|
| Headline / reframe opener | Playfair Display | Bold |
| Body copy / explanations | DM Sans | Regular |
| CTA text | DM Sans | Medium |
| Hero stats / hero numbers | Space Grotesk | Bold |
| Slide labels / micro-copy | DM Sans | Regular |

**Rules:**
- Max 40 words of body copy per slide.
- Numbers and stats always use Space Grotesk Bold.
- Final slide of every carousel: Playfair Display for the reframe, DM Sans for the CTA.
- No italic text. No decorative scripts.

### Layout Rules

- Canvas: 1080 × 1080 px (square, Instagram native)
- Safe zone margin: 80px all sides
- Account handle: bottom-right corner, every slide — `@fondapp` in DM Sans Regular, 14–16pt, muted (70% opacity)
- Slide 1 is always DARK BG
- Final slide is always DARK BG
- Dark/light background alternation every 2–3 slides
- No lifestyle photography, no faces, no people cooking
- Illustration style: flat, editorial, diagrammatic — NOT food-blog aesthetic

## SECTION 2: CONTINUOUS VISUAL THREAD SYSTEM

Every carousel has a single design element that progresses across all slides and completes on the final slide. This drives swipe-through rate by creating visual momentum — each new slide feels like arriving somewhere, not just reading another frame.

**Thread rules:**
- Occupies a consistent position on every slide (same corner or edge)
- Progresses in one direction — fills, grows, advances, or transforms
- Maximum 15–20% of slide area
- Never competes with text — keep it below or beside the primary content zone
- Completes/resolves on the final slide, timed to the CTA

### CAROUSEL #57 — "The Wrong Drawer Is Killing Your Herbs"

**Pillar:** Store It Right + Before It Goes Bad (Pillar 3)
**CTA Tier:** Tier 2
**Format:** Carousel · 7 slides
**Post Day:** Friday Week 19
**Post #:** 57

**THE ARC:** The 3-day death → why the crisper is wrong → the flower method (soft herbs) → the towel method (hard herbs) → the one herb that hates the fridge → the freeze-in-oil backup → reframe + CTA

**CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A single herb sprig in the bottom-center of every slide. On Slide 1 it lies limp and brown-edged on a bare surface. As the carousel progresses it is placed in a glass, the water rises, and the sprig straightens and greens until, by the final slide, it stands fully upright and vivid in a jar. The thread is the literal promise of the carousel: this is reversible.

---

**Slide 1 — DARK BG**

> You bought fresh herbs Sunday.
> They were slime by Wednesday.
> It was never the herbs. It was the drawer.

*Typography:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White. Three lines, vertically stacked, each landing separately. The third line is the accusation that reframes the first two. No stat on this slide — the claim is the visual.

*Visual:* Almost entirely typographic on Slate Black. Lower-center: a flat illustration of a limp parsley/cilantro sprig lying flat on a shelf, edges browned, one leaf curling. It looks defeated. This is the thread element in its start state — no glass yet, just the herb abandoned on a surface.

*Thread state:* Wilted sprig lying flat, ~12% of slide area, bottom-center. Brown edges, slumped. No water, no glass.

*Source:* Storage/spoilage framing from fond Pillar 3 reference (cut-herb handling, ice-water revival).

---

**Slide 2 — LIGHT BG**

> The crisper drawer is built for vegetables — sealed, still, humid.
>
> Cut herbs are not vegetables. They're cut stems. Sealed in a bag, they suffocate and rot in days.

*Typography:* Body in DM Sans Regular, Slate Black. "They're cut stems." in DM Sans Medium — the structural insight set apart with extra line-height.

*Visual:* A simple split diagram. Left half: a closed crisper drawer with a herb bag inside, three small "X" marks and a faint Signal Red haze around the bag (suffocation). Right half: the same herbs, stems trimmed, shown as what they actually are — a tiny bouquet of cut stems. A thin arrow points from "vegetable storage" (left) to "✗ wrong for this" label in Signal Red. Diagrammatic, not photographic.

*Thread state:* The sprig is now picked up off the surface — held at a slight angle, still wilted but no longer lying flat. An empty glass appears beside it, bottom-center.

---

**Slide 3 — LIGHT BG**

> SOFT HERBS — treat them like flowers.
>
> Basil, cilantro, parsley, mint, dill: trim the stems, stand them in an inch of water, loose bag over the top, into the fridge. They last 2–3 weeks.

*Typography:* "SOFT HERBS" label in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue. Body in DM Sans Regular. "2–3 weeks" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue (safe extension, not danger).

*Visual:* The hero method illustration — a drinking glass with ~1 inch of water (Steel Blue fill), a bunch of soft herbs standing upright in it like a flower vase, a loose translucent bag tented over the top. To the right, a small labeled list of the five soft herbs, each with a tiny leaf icon. This is the save-magnet slide — clean enough to read at thumbnail. The "flower" framing should be unmistakable.

*Thread state:* The sprig is now standing in the glass; water fills the bottom inch (Steel Blue). The sprig is half-revived — still a little droopy at the tip but upright.

---

**Slide 4 — DARK BG**

> HARD HERBS — treat them like delicate laundry.
>
> Rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage: wrap loosely in a slightly damp paper towel, then into a container or loose bag in the fridge. They last 2–3 weeks too.

*Typography:* "HARD HERBS" label in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue. Body in DM Sans Regular, Off-White. "2–3 weeks" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue.

*Visual:* A second method illustration on Slate Black, visually distinct from Slide 3 so the swipe feels like arrival. A flat-lay: woody herb sprigs (rosemary, thyme) lying on an open damp paper towel, the towel being folded over them like wrapping. A small water-droplet icon in Warm Gold indicates "slightly damp, not wet." Beside it, the four hard herbs listed with tiny sprig icons. The contrast with the "flowers" method on Slide 3 is the teaching device — two herb types, two methods.

*Thread state:* The sprig in the glass continues to recover — noticeably greener, standing taller. Water level holds.

---

**Slide 5 — LIGHT BG**

> One exception: basil hates the cold.
>
> Refrigerate basil and it blackens. Keep it on the counter in water, like a bouquet, away from direct sun.

*Typography:* Headline-ish opener "One exception: basil hates the cold." in DM Sans Medium, Slate Black. Body in DM Sans Regular. "basil" rendered in Warm Gold as the single highlighted term.

*Visual:* A single focused illustration: a glass of basil on a countertop (not in a fridge), a small sun icon to the side with a "✗" through direct contact. A side-by-side micro-comparison underneath — "fridge basil" (a blackened leaf, Signal Red) vs "counter basil" (a vivid green leaf, Warm Gold). The one counterintuitive rule, isolated so it sticks.

*Thread state:* The sprig is now almost fully upright and green — only the very tip still hints at its earlier wilt.

---

**Slide 6 — DARK BG**

> Already wilting? Two rescues:
>
> 1. Ice-water bath — limp greens and soft herbs perk back up in 15–30 minutes.
> 2. Blend the rest with olive oil, freeze in an ice-cube tray. Instant flavor bombs for months.

*Typography:* Opener in DM Sans Medium, Off-White. The two numbered rescues in DM Sans Regular; the lead verbs ("Ice-water bath", "Blend the rest") in Space Grotesk Bold, Warm Gold (these are the saves). "months" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue.

*Visual:* Two side-by-side mini-panels on Slate Black. Panel 1: a bowl of ice water with herbs submerged, a small clock reading "15–30 min", a curved Warm Gold "revival" arrow showing a limp leaf becoming firm. Panel 2: a blender pouring green herb-oil into an ice-cube tray, three cubes already frozen green. This is the "screenshot for later" slide — both rescues legible at thumbnail.

*Thread state:* The sprig stands fully upright now, vivid green — fully revived, mirroring the rescue message. Water jar full.

*Source:* Ice-water revival + herb-oil freezing from fond Pillar 3/4 reference.

---

**Slide 7 (Final — CTA Tier 2) — DARK BG**

> Your herbs weren't fragile.
> They were just in the wrong place.
>
> fond tells you what's about to turn — and how to store it before it does. Link in bio.

*Typography:* Playfair Display Bold for the two-line reframe, Off-White. "wrong place" in Warm Gold. DM Sans Medium for the CTA line in Steel Blue. "@fondapp" faint bottom-right.

*Visual:* Text-primary on Slate Black. The thread completes: the herb sprig now stands tall, green, and thriving in a clean glass jar of water, bottom-center — the visual opposite of Slide 1. A faint Warm Gold glow behind the jar marks it as the "rescued" end state. No competing elements.

*Thread state:* Fully resolved — upright, green, thriving in water. The before/after of Slide 1 → Slide 7 is the entire argument: nothing was wrong with the herbs.

### CAROUSEL #58 — "5 Things You Didn't Know You Could Freeze"

**Pillar:** Waste Hacks (Pillar 4)
**CTA Tier:** Tier 1
**Format:** Carousel · 6 slides
**Post Day:** Sunday Week 19
**Post #:** 58

**THE ARC:** The hook (your freezer is an escape hatch you're not using) → five rapid-fire reveals (wine, ginger, eggs, yogurt, herbs) → reframe + soft CTA

> Pillar 4 note: punchy, rapid-fire, lighter production bar. One surprising freezable item per slide, each with the *how*, not just the *what*. High comment-bait ("wait, you can freeze WINE?"). High Pinterest save potential.

**CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A 5-compartment ice-cube tray runs along the bottom edge of every slide as a full-width bar. It starts empty on Slide 1. Each reveal slide fills exactly one more compartment with a color-coded contents icon, so the tray doubles as a progress bar AND a recap. By the final slide all five are full — the viewer sees the complete set at a glance.

---

**Slide 1 — DARK BG**

> Your freezer is a pause button.
> You're using it for ice and not much else.
> Here are 5 things you didn't know you could freeze.

*Typography:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White, three stacked lines. "5" in Space Grotesk Bold, Warm Gold — the only colored element, signaling a countable list ahead.

*Visual:* Mostly typographic on Slate Black. Lower third: a flat illustration of a freezer drawer, mostly empty except a lonely ice tray — visually making the "you're barely using this" point. The emptiness is the joke.

*Thread state:* The 5-compartment tray sits empty along the bottom edge, thin outline in Off-White. All five compartments unfilled.

*Source:* Freezable-item list from fond Pillar 4 reference (eggs, ginger, wine, yogurt, herbs in oil).

---

**Slide 2 — LIGHT BG**

> 1. WINE.
> Leftover wine → freeze in an ice-cube tray. Drop a cube straight into sauces, braises, and pan deglazes. Never pour the last of the bottle out again.

*Typography:* "1. WINE." in Space Grotesk Bold, Slate Black, large. Body in DM Sans Regular. "ice-cube tray" in Steel Blue.

*Visual:* One clean object illustration: a wine bottle tipping a deep-red pour into an ice-cube tray, two cubes already frozen (deep burgundy). A small skillet icon to the side with a single cube dropping in. Single-idea slide — uncluttered, scroll-native.

*Thread state:* Tray compartment 1 fills — a burgundy cube icon. 1 of 5.

---

**Slide 3 — DARK BG**

> 2. FRESH GINGER.
> Freeze the whole knob. Grate it straight from frozen — no peeling, no stringy mess, and it lasts for months instead of shriveling in the drawer.

*Typography:* "2. FRESH GINGER." in Space Grotesk Bold, Off-White. Body in DM Sans Regular. "grate it straight from frozen" in Warm Gold (the trick). "months" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue.

*Visual:* A ginger knob illustration with a microplane grater shown mid-grate, fine shavings falling. A small "❄ → 🫚" sequence shows it goes in whole, comes out gratable. Contrast against the dark background makes it pop after the light Slide 2.

*Thread state:* Compartment 2 fills — a pale-gold ginger icon. 2 of 5.

---

**Slide 4 — LIGHT BG**

> 3. EGGS.
> Not in the shell — crack them first. Whisk, pour into a tray, freeze. Perfect for baking and scrambles. Yolks and whites freeze separately too.

*Typography:* "3. EGGS." in Space Grotesk Bold, Slate Black. Body in DM Sans Regular. "Not in the shell" in Signal Red (the mistake to avoid — a genuine "don't ruin your freezer" warning). "crack them first" in Steel Blue.

*Visual:* A cracked egg being whisked, poured into a tray compartment, one frozen yellow egg cube beside it. A small shelled egg with a Signal Red "✗" reinforces the one rule that matters. Clean, single-action illustration.

*Thread state:* Compartment 3 fills — a yellow egg cube icon. 3 of 5.

---

**Slide 5 — DARK BG**

> 4. GREEK YOGURT.
> About to expire? Freeze dollops on a tray. Drop them frozen into smoothies, or thaw for baking. Texture shifts slightly — flavor doesn't.
>
> 5. FRESH HERBS.
> Chop, pack into a tray, top with olive oil, freeze. Sauté-ready flavor cubes that last for months.

*Typography:* "4. GREEK YOGURT." and "5. FRESH HERBS." in Space Grotesk Bold, Off-White. Bodies in DM Sans Regular. "olive oil" and "smoothies" in Steel Blue. This slide carries the final two reveals together to keep the carousel tight.

*Visual:* A clean two-row split on Slate Black. Top row: a yogurt tub spooning white dollops onto a tray + a smoothie glass. Bottom row: chopped green herbs packed into tray cells with olive-oil drizzle (Warm Gold), three frozen green-flecked cubes. Two ideas, clearly divided by a thin Off-White rule.

*Thread state:* Compartments 4 and 5 both fill — a white yogurt cube and a green herb-oil cube. Tray now 5 of 5 — full.

---

**Slide 6 (Final — CTA Tier 1) — DARK BG**

> The freezer doesn't stop time.
> But it buys you weeks you were throwing away.
>
> We built an app that tracks what's about to expire — so you can freeze it in time. Link in bio.

*Typography:* Playfair Display Bold for the two-line reframe, Off-White. "weeks you were throwing away" — "throwing away" in Warm Gold. DM Sans Medium for the CTA in Steel Blue. No product name (Tier 1). "@fondapp" faint bottom-right.

*Visual:* Text-primary on Slate Black. The thread resolves: the full 5-compartment tray lifts from the bottom bar to center-stage, all five color-coded cubes glowing softly (burgundy, gold, yellow, white, green) — a complete, satisfying set the viewer can screenshot as the recap. A faint Warm Gold underglow marks the "saved" payoff.

*Thread state:* Tray complete and elevated — the five cubes are the recap and the reward.

**Post Day:** Monday Week 20

**CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A vertical 5-tier shelf rack stands along the right edge of every slide. ## SECTION 6: DESIGN PRINCIPLES FOR THIS BRIEF

1. **The thread IS the argument, every time.** Post 57's reviving herb, Post 58's filling tray, and Post 59's climbing tag each carry the editorial point on their own. They should read even with the text muted.
2. **Signal Red only for loss.** Use it on Post 58's "not in the shell" warning, Post 59's premium-zone and the $115–$120 bar, and Post 57's suffocation haze. Never as decoration. Savings and safe-extension numbers are Steel Blue.
3. **Save-magnet slides must be thumbnail-legible.** Post 57 Slide 3 (flower method) and Slide 6 (two rescues); Post 58 Slide 6 (full-tray recap); Post 59 Slide 6 (unit-price card). These get the saves — prioritize legibility over flourish.
4. **Two-method contrast teaches.** Post 57 Slides 3 vs 4 (soft vs hard herbs) must look visibly different — one "flowers in water," one "wrapped in towel." The contrast is the lesson.
5. **Pillar 4 stays light and fast.** Post 58 is punchy and rapid-fire — one clean object per reveal, no dense diagrams. It should feel like a quick flip, not a lecture.
6. **Pillar 1 earns trust, doesn't sell.** Post 59's CTA is Tier 1 — soft, no product name. The outrage does the work; the app is a quiet "by the way."
7. **No food photography, no faces, no styled shots.** All food and shelf elements are flat, editorial, diagrammatic illustrations. Silhouettes for shoppers are abstract, never detailed people.
8. **Dark/light rhythm holds.** Slide 1 and final slide always dark; alternate every 2–3 slides in between as specced per carousel.
