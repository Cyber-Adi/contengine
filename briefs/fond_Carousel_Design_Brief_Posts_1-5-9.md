# SECTION 1: BRAND SYSTEM

## Color Palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Dark BG** | `#0F0F0F` | Primary dark slide background |
| **Deep Charcoal** | `#1A1A1A` | Alternate dark background (use when Dark BG feels too stark) |
| **Warm Off-White** | `#F5F0EB` | Light slide background |
| **Forge White** | `#FFFFFF` | Headlines, primary text on dark backgrounds |
| **Signal Red** | `#E63329` | LOSS and WASTE statistics ONLY — never decorative |
| **Warm Amber** | `#E89040` | Featured hero stats, key callout numbers |
| **Fresh Green** | `#2D7A45` | Savings, solutions, "correct" answers |
| **Tag Yellow** | `#F5D84F` | Price tag elements, retail callouts (Pillar 1 only) |
| **Muted Body** | `#C8C3BC` | Body copy on dark backgrounds |
| **Quiet Gray** | `#888480` | Captions, source citations, secondary text |
| **Red Tint Surface** | `#1C0A08` | Slide backgrounds when the slide's primary emotion is loss/outrage |

## Typography

| Role | Font | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Post Title / Slide 1 Hook** | Space Grotesk | Bold (700) | 44–60px on 1080×1080 canvas |
| **Hero Numbers / Stats** | Space Grotesk | ExtraBold (800) or Black (900) | Fill 40–60% of slide width |
| **Slide Body Copy** | Space Grotesk | Medium (500) | Max 40 words per slide |
| **Labels / Annotations** | Inter | Regular (400) | Used for source citations, diagram labels |
| **Source Citations** | Inter | Light (300) or Regular (400) | Small, `#888480`, bottom-right of slide |

**Rules:**
- Never mix more than 2 font weights on one slide
- All numbers/statistics use Space Grotesk, never Inter
- Source citations always appear on the slide where the stat is used — never on a separate "sources" slide

## Layout Grid

- **Canvas:** 1080 × 1080 px (Instagram square)
- **Safe zone margins:** 80px on all sides for text
- **Thread strip:** 15% of slide height (162px) reserved at the bottom for the continuous visual thread
- **Slide 1 always DARK BG**
- **Final slide always DARK BG**
- **Background alternation:** Every 2–3 slides; never more than 3 consecutive slides of the same BG type

## Visual Language Rules

1. **NO stock photography of people.** No cooking hands, no family dinners, no styled lifestyle kitchens.
2. **NO photography at all unless it's a product close-up** (price tag, food package, receipt). Even then, desaturate or treat it as a graphic element.
3. **YES to diagrams, flat illustrations, data visualizations, infographics, and typographic emphasis.**
4. **Signal Red is sacred.** It appears ONLY when a number or fact represents money lost, food wasted, or a system designed against the viewer. Do not use it for decoration, borders, or general emphasis.
5. **Every slide must feel like arriving somewhere new.** Each swipe should register as a visual shift — new layout, new primary element, different spatial arrangement.
6. **Reference-card slides** (grids, cheat sheets, comparison tables) should be designed to be screenshot-worthy. Clean lines, high contrast, scannable.

# SECTION 2: CONTINUOUS VISUAL THREAD SYSTEM

A continuous visual thread is a design element that **occupies the same position on every slide** and **progresses forward** — filling, building, transforming, or revealing — so that the viewer subconsciously tracks their progress through the carousel.

**Rules:**
- Occupies the bottom 15% of the slide (the thread strip) — `y: 918px to 1080px`
- Progresses in one direction only: forward, filling, or growing
- Completes on the final slide, coinciding with the CTA or reframe moment
- Never competes with body copy — the thread is background-level, not foreground
- Total thread area: 1080 × 162 px strip

**This batch uses 3 different thread types — one per carousel:**

| Post | Thread Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Post #1 | **Price Tag Strip** | A row of 6 shelf price tags. Each slide "stamps" one tag with a red PAID mark. By Slide 7, all 6 non-eye-level slots are marked. The eye-level one gets a dollar sign. |
| Post #5 | **Depleting Dollar** | A stylized $100 bill graphic in the strip. Each slide tears away a piece. By Slide 7, only the shredded remnant remains with "$2,913/yr" printed on it. |
| Post #9 | **Food Migration Strip** | 7 food icons (tomato, banana, potato, onion, garlic, avocado, + a fridge icon at far right). Each slide moves one food icon from the fridge side to the counter side, turning it green. Starts with all 7 on the fridge (red). Ends with all 7 on the counter (green). |

## CAROUSEL #1 — "The Shelf Is Rigged"

**Pillar:** Supermarket Secrets (Pillar 1)
**Post #:** 1 (First Monday post — acquisition, trust-building)
**CTA Tier:** Tier 0 — Zero CTA. No mention of the account, no product reference, pure value.
**Format:** Carousel · 7 slides
**Platform:** Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest

**THE ARC:** `Curiosity → Proof → The Math → The System → The Scale → Complicity → The Counter-Move`
The viewer arrives thinking they make their own choices. By Slide 6, they understand the entire aisle is a paid battlefield. Slide 7 gives them one actionable counter-move so they leave with agency, not helplessness.

**CONTINUOUS THREAD — Price Tag Strip:**
- **Element:** A horizontal strip of 6 small shelf price tags (white rectangles with red borders, dollar amounts hidden), arranged left to right in the bottom 162px strip.
- **Start state (Slide 1):** All 6 tags are blank/unlit. They look like neutral shelf labels.
- **Progression:** Each slide from Slide 2 onward stamps one tag RED with the word "PAID." The tags fill left to right.
- **End state (Slide 7):** All 6 tags show "PAID" in red. One additional tag — positioned in the center/eye-level position — glows amber with a "$" symbol. The contrast makes the point: the whole shelf is paid for.
- **Position:** Bottom-center, spanning full width. Tags are 140 × 100px each, separated by 20px gaps.

## CAROUSEL #5 — "The Number"

**Pillar:** The $2,913 Problem (Pillar 2)
**Post #:** 5 (First Wednesday post — first Pillar 2 hit, statistics and recognition)
**CTA Tier:** Tier 0 — Zero CTA. No product reference. Trust-building first.
**Format:** Carousel · 7 slides
**Platform:** Instagram, TikTok

**THE ARC:** `Shock → Credibility → Breakdown → Historical Dread → The $56 Gut Punch → The Invisible Pattern → Recognition`
Slide 1 drops the number with no context. The audience either doesn't believe it or is stunned. Each subsequent slide makes it more believable, more personal, and more inescapable. Slide 7 reframes: the problem isn't behavior, it's visibility.

**CONTINUOUS THREAD — Depleting Dollar:**
- **Element:** A simplified $100 bill illustration (flat, stylized — not a photographic reproduction of actual currency) rendered in the bottom 162px strip. It spans the full slide width.
- **Start state (Slide 1):** The bill is intact. "$100" visible on it in clean type.
- **Progression:** Each slide tears away a piece from the right end of the bill. The tear line is jagged, imprecise — like it was actually torn. The torn portion becomes dark/empty space. The remaining bill piece is full-color.
- **End state (Slide 7):** Almost entirely torn. Only a tattered fragment remains in the lower-left, showing "$2,913/yr" printed on it. The rest is dark empty space.
- **Position:** Bottom strip, full-width. The bill height fills the full 162px strip.

### SLIDE 1 — The Number

**Background:** Dark BG (`#0F0F0F`)

**Copy:**
> $2,913.
> That's how much your family throws away in food every year.

**Typography:**
- "$2,913." — Space Grotesk Black, Forge White (`#FFFFFF`), **90–100px** — this is the largest text in the entire brief. It should feel slightly too large. Uncomfortable in its size.
- "That's how much your family throws away in food every year." — Space Grotesk Medium, Muted Body (`#C8C3BC`), 26px — sits below the number with significant white space between

**Visual Description:**
The number is the entire slide. "$2,913." centered at approximately 40% down from the top of the safe zone. Below it, generous white space, then the supporting line in muted text. Nothing else. No illustration, no diagram, no secondary element. The dark background makes the white number feel like it's floating in space. The point is isolation: this number deserves all available attention. No thread element competes.

**Thread state:** Dollar bill visible, intact, at full opacity. The viewer will barely notice it yet.

# SECTION 6: DESIGN PRINCIPLES

These rules govern ALL fond carousel content. Non-negotiable.

1. **Signal Red is earned.** It appears only when a number or fact represents money lost, food wasted, or a system designed against the viewer. Use it at most once or twice per carousel. Never on a background (except `#1C0A08` which is a dark tint, not Signal Red).

2. **The swipe must feel like progress.** Every slide should feel spatially different from the one before it. Change the layout axis (horizontal → vertical → centered), the BG type, or the primary visual element class (diagram → number → illustration → comparison) at least once every 2 slides.

3. **Reference slides should look like they belong in a textbook.** High save rate comes from utility. If a slide contains a comparison, checklist, or framework — design it so it can be screenshot and immediately understood without context.

4. **Illustrations are flat and purposeful.** No gradients, no shadows on illustrated elements, no 3D effects. The illustrative style is clean flat design — lines and fills only.

5. **Slide 1 always dark. Final slide always dark.** The arc opens in darkness (attention, weight) and closes in darkness (impact, memorability). Light slides in the middle provide relief and visual breathing room.

6. **Thread subtlety.** The thread strip should be noticed on the second or third swipe, not the first. If the thread is competing with the copy on any slide, it is too prominent. Reduce opacity or simplify the illustration.

7. **No people. No lifestyle.** This account's visual identity is educational, not aspirational. No kitchen photography, no family moments, no food blog aesthetics.

8. **The copy is the spec.** Do not paraphrase or edit the copy during design execution. If copy feels too long for a slide, the layout must adapt — never cut words.
