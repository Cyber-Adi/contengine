## SECTION 1: BRAND SYSTEM

### Color Palette

| Role | Name | Hex |
|------|------|-----|
| Dark background | Slate Black | `#0A0A0A` |
| Light background | Off-White | `#F5F1E8` |
| Primary text (on dark) | Off-White | `#F5F1E8` |
| Primary text (on light) | Slate Black | `#0A0A0A` |
| Highlight / CTA / key words | Steel Blue | `#4A7FAC` |
| Waste / loss / negative data | Signal Red | `#D93A2B` |
| Emotional accent / warmth | Warm Gold | `#D4A843` |

**Rules:**
- Signal Red is used ONLY for waste, loss, or negative data. Never decoratively.
- Warm Gold is used for emotional beats, key reframe words, and the CTA callback element.
- Steel Blue is the app's voice — used on CTAs, reframe words, and featured terms.
- Never use gradient backgrounds or drop shadows.

### Typography

| Role | Font | Weight |
|------|------|--------|
| Headline / reframe opener | Playfair Display | Bold |
| Body copy / explanations | DM Sans | Regular |
| CTA text | DM Sans | Medium |
| Hero stats / hero numbers | Space Grotesk | Bold |
| Slide labels / micro-copy | DM Sans | Regular |

**Rules:**
- Max 40 words of body copy per slide.
- Numbers and stats always use Space Grotesk Bold.
- Final slide of every carousel: Playfair Display for the reframe, DM Sans for the CTA.
- No italic text. No decorative scripts.

### Layout Rules

- Canvas: 1080 × 1080 px (square, Instagram native)
- Safe zone margin: 80px all sides
- Account handle: bottom-right corner, every slide — `@fondapp` in DM Sans Regular, 14–16pt, muted (70% opacity)
- Slide 1 is always DARK BG
- Final slide is always DARK BG
- Dark/light background alternation every 2–3 slides
- No lifestyle photography, no faces, no people cooking
- Illustration style: flat, editorial, diagrammatic — NOT food-blog aesthetic

## SECTION 2: CONTINUOUS VISUAL THREAD SYSTEM

Every carousel has a single design element that progresses across all slides and completes on the final slide. This drives swipe-through rate by creating visual momentum — each new slide feels like arriving somewhere, not just reading another frame.

**Thread rules:**
- Occupies a consistent position on every slide (same corner or edge)
- Progresses in one direction — fills, grows, advances, or transforms
- Maximum 15–20% of slide area
- Never competes with text — keep it below or beside the primary content zone
- Completes/resolves on the final slide, timed to the CTA

|--------|-------|---------------|-------------|-----------|----------|

**Thread description:** A rectangular "BEST BY MM/DD/YY" sticker — red border, white center, printed in a clinical font — positioned in the bottom-left corner of every slide. On Slide 1, the sticker is pristine and complete. Each subsequent slide, the top-right corner curls back slightly more, revealing the smooth food surface beneath (Off-White). By Slide 6, the sticker is nearly fully peeled, hanging by a corner. On Slide 7 (final), the sticker is gone — only a faint rectangular outline remains where it was. The food beneath is unmarked, intact, perfectly fine. The visual argument across the carousel: the label was never telling you the truth about the food.

### CAROUSEL #56 — "'Best By' Is Not an Expiration Date"

**Pillar:** The $2,913 Problem (Pillar 2)
**CTA Tier:** Tier 2
**Format:** Carousel · 7 slides
**Post Day:** Wednesday Week 19
**Post #:** 56

**THE ARC:** The shocking number → three labels decoded → who benefits from the confusion → the dollar cost of your confusion → the real test → the most over-tossed foods → the reframe

**CONTINUOUS THREAD:** "BEST BY" sticker in bottom-left corner, progressively peeling back across slides. Starts pristine on Slide 1. Fully removed by Slide 7. See Section 2 for full description.

---

**Slide 1 — DARK BG**

> 80% of Americans have thrown away
> food based on a date the government
> didn't set.

*Typography:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White. Three lines, vertically centered. Each line lands separately — the third is the gut-punch that reframes the previous two. No subtitle, no stat in this slide. The claim is the visual.

*Visual:* The composition is almost entirely typographic. In the lower-center of the slide, a single illustration: a full milk carton slightly tipped toward the viewer, a "BEST BY" date label prominently visible on its side. The label is drawn in crisp Signal Red border with white interior — clinical, official-looking. The carton is the only illustration on the slide; the rest is text and Slate Black background. The sticker's authority contrasts with the headline's accusation.

*Thread state:* Sticker is pristine — full sticker, red border intact, date fully visible. No curl. Positioned bottom-left, roughly 12% of slide area.

*Source:* NRDC "The Dating Game" report, 2013 (updated figures cited in 2022 ReFED Insights Engine).

---

**Slide 2 — LIGHT BG**

> There are three different date labels. They mean three different things. None of them mean "unsafe after this date."
>
> **Best By** — quality estimate. Made by the manufacturer.
> **Sell By** — stock rotation instruction. For the store, not you.
> **Use By** — the closest to a real deadline. Still usually conservative.

*Typography:* Intro sentence in DM Sans Regular, Slate Black. The three labels in Space Grotesk Bold, Slate Black — left-aligned, with definitions in DM Sans Regular beside them. "Best By," "Sell By," "Use By" are the three label names — each on its own row, bold, treated as a classification system.

*Visual:* A three-row comparison table, styled as a decoder card. Left column: the label name in Space Grotesk Bold. Center: a small illustration of the label type (a product stamp, a shelf edge tag, and a sealed-package seal respectively). Right column: the one-sentence truth in DM Sans Regular. Each row has a subtle left border — neutral gray for "Best By," neutral gray for "Sell By," Steel Blue for "Use By" (the one that actually matters). The card format is designed for screenshots.

*Thread state:* Sticker corner just beginning to curl — top-right corner lifted by ~3mm, revealing a thin sliver of Off-White beneath.

---

**Slide 3 — LIGHT BG**

> The US has no federal law requiring date labels on most foods. The FDA encouraged "Best If Used By" as a standard in 2017. The food industry ignored it. Ambiguity is profitable.
>
> When you throw food away early, you buy more sooner.

*Typography:* Body in DM Sans Regular. Final line — "When you throw food away early, you buy more sooner." — in DM Sans Medium, Slate Black, set apart with extra line-height. It's not a statistic. It's the logic. The weight distinction signals this is the structural argument.

*Visual:* A cause-and-effect chain diagram running left to right across the slide. Icon 1: a food label with "BEST BY" (red). Arrow. Icon 2: a confused face silhouette (simple, abstract). Arrow. Icon 3: a trash can (Signal Red). Arrow. Icon 4: a shopping cart (Steel Blue). The chain ends with a coin dropping into a corporate building outline — representing the brand that sold the replacement product. Below the chain: a single callout box reading "legally required on: baby formula only." The exception makes the rule visible.

*Thread state:* Sticker peeling — top-right corner curled back about 20%, a small half-circle of Off-White now visible beneath.

---

**Slide 4 — DARK BG**

> Date label confusion causes an estimated **20% of all household food waste** in the US.
>
> On a $2,913 annual waste figure, that's **$580/year** thrown away because of a label that has no legal definition.

*Typography:* Intro sentence in DM Sans Regular, Off-White. "20%" in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red — hero-sized, taking up substantial vertical space. Below it: "$580/year" in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red. The two numbers are the slide. Everything else is context.

*Visual:* The slide is built around a pie chart representing the $2,913 annual household food waste figure. The full circle is Slate Black outline on Slate Black — barely visible. One slice (20%) is filled in Signal Red. The slice is labeled "date confusion" in DM Sans Regular, Off-White. All other slices are unlabeled and in muted dark gray — they exist to give the 20% proportion visual meaning, not to explain them. Below the chart: "$580/year — the cheapest problem to fix" in DM Sans Regular at 70% opacity. The smallest but most actionable slice.

*Thread state:* Sticker peeling — approximately 40% lifted, the curl is now clearly dramatic. The bottom portion of the sticker is still adhered; the top half flaps free.

*Source:* ReFED Insights Engine (2022); NRDC "The Dating Game" (2013, figures replicated in subsequent studies).

---

**Slide 5 — LIGHT BG**

> The actual test for whether food is safe has nothing to do with a label.
>
> **Smell it.** Off means off — your nose is reliable.
> **Look at it.** Mold, slime, or discoloration = toss.
> **Texture check.** Mushy where it should be firm = gone.
>
> If it passes all three: it's fine.

*Typography:* Intro in DM Sans Regular. The three tests in Space Grotesk Bold (just the bold lead word: "Smell it." / "Look at it." / "Texture check.") followed by DM Sans Regular explanation. "If it passes all three: it's fine." in DM Sans Medium, Steel Blue — the reassurance line. It earns the scroll.

*Visual:* Three horizontal rows, each containing a simple sensory icon on the left + the rule on the right. Row 1: a nose icon → "Off smell = toss. No smell = fine." Row 2: an eye icon → "Mold / slime / discoloration = toss." Row 3: a hand/finger icon → "Mushy where firm = gone. Firm = fine." Each icon is in Warm Gold — these are your tools, not warnings. A green checkmark on the far right of each row appears for the "passes" condition. The layout is a decision framework — this is the slide people save and reference.

*Thread state:* Sticker peeling — approximately 60% lifted. The sticker is clearly in the process of being removed. The food surface beneath is visible and unmarked.

---

**Slide 6 — LIGHT BG**

> The most over-tossed foods in American kitchens — and how long they actually last after the label date:
>
> Milk — 5–7 days past "Sell By"
> Hard cheese — 3–4 weeks past "Best By"
> Greek yogurt — 2–3 weeks past "Best By"
> Bread — 5–7 days past "Best By" (or freeze it)
> Dry pasta — 1–2 years past "Best By"
> Eggs — 3–5 weeks after purchase

*Typography:* Intro in DM Sans Regular. Each item in Space Grotesk Bold (food name) + DM Sans Regular (timeframe). The list is tight and scannable. "Dry pasta — 1–2 years" should be the visual standout — that one always breaks people.

*Visual:* A reference grid — 2 columns × 3 rows — each cell containing a flat illustration of the food item (milk carton, cheese wedge, yogurt tub, bread loaf, pasta box, egg) plus the food name and the real timeframe. Each cell has a thin Off-White border on the light background. The timeframes are in Steel Blue (they represent safe extension, not danger). This slide is a cheat sheet — it is designed to be screenshotted. The layout should be clean enough to read at 50% thumbnail size. No decorative elements.

*Thread state:* Sticker nearly fully peeled — only the bottom-left corner remains adhered. The sticker hangs at a dramatic angle. The food beneath is almost fully revealed.

*Sources:* FDA Refrigerator & Freezer Storage Chart (standing); USDA FoodKeeper App data; food science consensus for dry pasta/cheese/eggs.

---

**Slide 7 (Final — CTA Tier 2) — DARK BG**

> The label tells you when the manufacturer stops
> being responsible for quality.
>
> It says nothing about when the food stops being safe.
>
> fond tracks real shelf life — not label dates. Link in bio.

*Typography:* Playfair Display Bold for the two-sentence reframe, Off-White. "Quality" in Warm Gold — the word the label is actually about, distinguished from "safe." Line break between the two sentences creates a beat. DM Sans Medium for the CTA line in Steel Blue. "@fondapp" handle faint in bottom-right.

*Visual:* Text-primary slide. The only illustration: where the sticker thread element was in the bottom-left, it is now completely gone. The space it occupied shows a faint rectangular outline — ghost of the sticker — in 20% opacity Off-White. Inside the ghost outline, in DM Sans Regular at 30% opacity: "quality estimate." The sticker was never what you thought it was. The ghost is the final visual argument. No other design elements compete with this.

*Thread state:* Sticker fully removed. Only the ghost rectangle remains where it was. The thread's completion is the visual argument of the entire carousel: the label was never telling you the truth about the food. Now it's gone, and the food — and you — are fine.

## SECTION 6: DESIGN PRINCIPLES FOR THIS BRIEF

1. **The thread IS the argument.** The sticker peeling back across 7 slides is the entire editorial point of the carousel. Don't reduce it to a decorative element — it should feel intentional and visible. On Slides 4 and 5, the curl should be dramatic enough to notice before reading the copy.

2. **The decoder card on Slide 2 is a save magnet.** Make it clean enough to read at 50% thumbnail. People screenshot and share decoder cards. This is a reference format — prioritize legibility over aesthetics.

3. **Signal Red is used aggressively here but correctly.** The 20% pie slice (Slide 4), the "80%" stat on Slide 1 context, and the "BEST BY" label borders are all Signal Red because they represent a system designed to create waste. This is the appropriate use.

4. **Slide 6 is the cheat sheet slide.** The 2×3 grid should be tight, clean, and legible at thumbnail. These are the exact items that trigger the most premature disposal. This slide will get more saves than any other in the carousel — it's actionable and specific.

5. **The ghost outline on Slide 7 is the most important detail.** It is subtle and it must be executed precisely — 20% opacity Off-White rectangle on Slate Black background, with the text "quality estimate" barely visible inside. If the designer is unsure whether it's visible enough, it's probably right. It should feel like a scar, not a label.

6. **No food photography. No styled shots.** All food items are flat illustrations — clean, editorial, diagram-quality. The carton, the cheese wedge, the pasta box. These are iconic representations of food categories, not appetizing imagery.

7. **Dry pasta line on Slide 6 is the standout.** "1–2 years past Best By" is the most shocking data point in the carousel. It should be visually distinguishable from the other items — slightly larger font or a thin highlight box around its cell. Not loud — just noticeably different.
