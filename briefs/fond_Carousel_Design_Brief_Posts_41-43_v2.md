## SECTION 1 — THE fond DESIGN SYSTEM (non-negotiable)

### 1.1 Brand character
fond's faceless account looks like **investigative journalism about your kitchen**: the authority of a financial broadsheet, the warmth of a well-lit pantry. Editorial, not lifestyle. Every slide is information design — a diagram, a number, a comparison, a reference card — never decoration. If a slide could appear in a food blogger's feed, it's wrong. If it could appear in a print explainer from The Economist about grocery stores, it's right.

### 1.2 Color palette (exact hex — no substitutions)

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Slate Black** | `#14161A` | Dark slide backgrounds. Near-black with a cool undertone; never pure #000. |
| **Off-White** | `#F5F2EC` | Light slide backgrounds + primary text on dark. Warm paper tone; never pure #FFF. |
| **fond Green** | `#2E5E4E` | The brand color. CTA cards, the word "fond," positive/solution states, green checkmarks. |
| **Steel Blue** | `#4A7BA6` | Secondary accent. Safe/good data states, key terms in reframes, CTA text on dark. |
| **Signal Red** | `#D64541` | Loss, waste, and cost ONLY. Never decorative. If it isn't a negative number or a warning, it isn't red. |
| **Harvest Gold** | `#D9A441` | Warm highlight. Sparingly: one emotional word per carousel max, thread elements, "caution" data states. |
| **Ink Gray** | `#6B7280` | Captions, source citations, secondary labels. |

Rules: max 3 accent colors per slide. Backgrounds are always Slate Black or Off-White — no gradients, no photos as backgrounds, no textures.

### 1.3 Typography

| Font | Weight | Role | Size @ 1080×1350 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Playfair Display** | Bold | Hooks (slides 1–2) and reframes (slides 7–8). The editorial voice. | 72–96pt hooks; 56–72pt reframes |
| **DM Sans** | Regular / Medium | All body copy, labels, CTAs. | 32–40pt body; 28pt labels |
| **Space Grotesk** | Bold | Every number, stat, dollar amount, percentage. Numbers are never set in the body font. | 80–140pt hero numbers; matches body size inline |
| **DM Sans** | Regular, Ink Gray | Source citations, bottom of stat slides. | 20–22pt |

Line length ≤ 8 words per line on hooks. Body copy ≤ 40 words per slide, ragged-left, never justified, never centered except hero numbers and CTA cards.

### 1.4 Layout grid
- Canvas: **1080 × 1350 px** (4:5 portrait), export PNG, sRGB.
- Margins: 96px all sides. Nothing but the continuous thread may touch the margin zone.
- Handle "@" placement: bottom-left corner inside margin, DM Sans 24pt, Ink Gray, every slide.
- Slide counter ("1/8"): bottom-right, same spec.
- The continuous thread occupies a consistent zone (bottom 15%, or one side rail) and never overlaps text.

### 1.5 Illustration & iconography
- **Flat vector illustration only.** 2px consistent stroke or filled shapes; no 3D, no drop shadows, no gradients, no photorealism.
- Food items are simplified, recognizable silhouettes with one accent color + Off-White/Slate line work.
- Icons: geometric, single-weight, one visual family per carousel (all outlined or all filled — never mixed).
- Diagrams are the hero: timelines, flow loops, bar comparisons, floor plans, split-frames, before/afters, pie-on-a-plate. Every diagram readable in under 2 seconds at phone size.
- **Metaphor objects** (receipts, textbook covers, price tags) are the signature reframing move — max one per carousel.

### 1.6 The fond CTA card (final slide, standardized)
- Background: fond Green `#2E5E4E`.
- "fond" in lowercase, DM Sans Medium, Off-White `#F5F2EC`, centered — the only time the wordmark appears.
- One line of CTA copy (Playfair for the reframe fragment, DM Sans for the ask).
- One send-trigger line in Off-White at 70% opacity.
- "Join the waitlist — link in bio" + a small down-arrow glyph. No logo, no app screenshots, no QR codes.

### 1.7 Never do
Stock photography or photorealistic people/kitchens · gradients, drop shadows, bevels · pure black or pure white backgrounds · Signal Red used decoratively · more than 40 words of body copy per slide · centered paragraph text · the same layout on two consecutive slides · the word "PantryPal."

## SECTION 2 — CONTINUOUS VISUAL THREAD SYSTEM

Every carousel has ONE design element that progresses across all 8 slides and completes on the final slide. It drives swipe-through — the viewer keeps going to see the element finish.

**Rules:**
- Occupies a consistent zone on every slide (bottom bar or side rail as specified below).
- Progresses in one direction only. Never resets, never reverses.
- Stays ≤ 15–20% of slide area. Never competes with or overlaps text.
- Completes on slide 8, coinciding with the fond CTA card.

**Thread assignments for this batch:**

| Carousel | Thread element | Start (slide 1) | End (slide 8) | Zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| #41 | Audio waveform slowing to flatline | Fast, tight waveform in Harvest Gold `#D9A441` | Flat horizontal line resolving into the underline of the fond wordmark | Bottom bar (bottom 12% of canvas) |
| #42 | Fridge shrinking, decade by decade | Oversized 22 cu ft modern fridge outline | Small 1950s fridge outline, door closing, fond Green fill | Bottom-right corner block |
| #43 | Thermometer mercury dropping 40°F → 37°F | Mercury at 40°F in Signal Red `#D64541` | Mercury at 37°F in Steel Blue `#4A7BA6`, tick turns to green check | Right side rail (right 12% of canvas) |

## CAROUSEL #41 — "The Music Is Slowing You Down"

**Pillar:** 1 — Supermarket Secrets · **CTA Tier:** 2 · **Slides:** 8 · **Post day:** Monday
**THE ARC:** Familiarity → suspicion → the experiment → the targeting → the full sensory map → the discipline → the pattern → the way out.
**CONTINUOUS THREAD:** An audio waveform runs the full width of the bottom bar on every slide. Slide 1: fast, tight, energetic peaks in Harvest Gold `#D9A441`. Each slide, the peaks stretch wider and lower (the tempo audibly "slowing" in visual form). Slide 8: the waveform is a single flat horizontal line that becomes the underline beneath the fond wordmark on the CTA card. Same vertical position every slide.

### Slide 1 — HOOK — DARK BG `#14161A`
> The music in the grocery store
> is chosen by a psychologist.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 84pt, Off-White `#F5F2EC`, ragged-left, ≤8 words/line.
- **Visual:** A single oversized music-note icon (flat, filled, Harvest Gold `#D9A441`) centered-right. A thin 2px Off-White line connects it downward to a small brain icon. Nothing else. The sparseness makes the claim feel authoritative.
- **Thread:** Waveform at maximum tempo — tight, tall peaks, Harvest Gold, bottom bar.

### Slide 2 — BACKUP HOOK — DARK BG `#14161A`
> You didn't decide to spend 40 minutes in there.
> The playlist decided for you.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 72pt, Off-White. "The playlist" in Harvest Gold `#D9A441` (the carousel's one gold emotional phrase).
- **Visual:** A clock face icon at left, its minute hand replaced by a music note. Standalone scroll-stopper — must work for a cold viewer who never saw slide 1 (Instagram re-serves slide 2 first in the 24–48h window).
- **Thread:** Peaks widen slightly — tempo dropping.

### Slide 3 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> A landmark study found slow-tempo store music increased time in-store by 38%. Shoppers browsed longer and spent 32% more per trip. Fast music reversed the effect.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black `#14161A`. Numbers "38%" and "32%" inline in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red `#D64541` (spending loss to the shopper).
- **Visual:** Two side-by-side panels. Left: slow, wide waveform in Harvest Gold, labeled "slow tempo → +38% time, +32% spending." Right: tight compressed waveform in Steel Blue `#4A7BA6`, labeled "fast tempo → faster trip, less spending." The waveform shapes make tempo visible without audio.
- **Source (DM Sans 20pt, Ink Gray `#6B7280`):** Milliman, Journal of Marketing, 1982 — replicated in later studies.
- **Thread:** Peaks now noticeably wider and lower.

### Slide 4 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> The genre matters too. Classical music in the wine aisle made shoppers pick pricier bottles. Country in a convenience store lengthened snack-aisle dwell time. The playlist is a targeting tool.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black.
- **Visual:** Two horizontal cause-effect chains, stacked. Row 1: violin icon → wine bottle icon → "higher price chosen" in Signal Red `#D64541`. Row 2: guitar icon → chip-bag icon → "longer browsing" in Signal Red. All icons outlined style, 2px stroke, Slate Black with one accent each.
- **Source:** Areni & Kim, Journal of Business Research, 1993.
- **Thread:** Continues widening.

### Slide 5 — VALUE — DARK BG `#14161A`
> Scent. Sound. Lighting. Temperature. Cart size. Every sensory input in the store has been tested, measured, and optimized for one variable: basket size.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Off-White. "basket size" in Space Grotesk Bold.
- **Visual:** A human silhouette (flat, Off-White outline) centered. Five labeled arrows point inward: "scent" (nose), "sound" (ear), "light" (eyes), "temperature" (skin), "touch" (cart handle). Each arrow a different muted palette color — sound's arrow is Harvest Gold, linking to the thread. The viewer is the target; the diagram makes that unavoidable.
- **Thread:** Peaks half their original height, stretched long.

### Slide 6 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> This isn't a conspiracy. It's a discipline called "retail atmospherics." Taught in business schools, published in journals, practiced by every major chain. The science is public. The application is invisible.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black.
- **Visual:** The carousel's metaphor object: a mock academic journal cover, cleanly ruled, Off-White page on the light background with a Slate Black border. Title in Playfair: "Atmospheric Effects on Shopping Behavior." Small caption line: "47 peer-reviewed papers since 1973" — "47" in Space Grotesk Bold.
- **Source:** Turley & Milliman, review of 47 studies, 2000.
- **Thread:** Nearly flat — two shallow bumps.

### Slide 7 — THE REFRAME — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> The store doesn't just sell you food.
> It sells you the mood to buy more of it.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 64pt, Slate Black. "mood" in Harvest Gold `#D9A441` — a deliberate color callback to the slide-1 music note.
- **Visual:** Text-dominant save-slide. Below the reframe, a single-row mini-summary strip: music note → clock → wine bottle → human silhouette → journal — the whole argument in five icons, Ink Gray, screenshot-ready.
- **Thread:** One last shallow ripple.

### Slide 8 — CTA (fond card) — fond Green BG `#2E5E4E`
> A list is immune to a playlist.
> fond builds yours from what your kitchen actually needs.
> Send this to whoever does the groceries.
> Join the waitlist — link in bio ↓

- **Layout per standardized fond CTA card (Section 1.6):** "fond" lowercase, DM Sans Medium, Off-White, centered. "A list is immune to a playlist." in Playfair Display, Off-White. CTA line in DM Sans. Send-trigger line in Off-White at 70% opacity.
- **Thread:** The waveform is now a perfectly flat line — and it IS the underline beneath the fond wordmark. The thread completes as the brand resolves the noise.

## CAROUSEL #42 — "Your Grandparents Wasted Almost Nothing"

**Pillar:** 2 — The $2,913 Problem · **CTA Tier:** 2 · **Slides:** 8 · **Post day:** Wednesday
**THE ARC:** The contrast → the sting → how they did it → what changed → the abundance trap → the irony → the constraints insight → the modern fix.
**CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A fridge outline in the bottom-right corner block shrinks slide by slide. Slide 1: a huge 22 cu ft double-door fridge outline (Off-White or Slate line, per background). Each slide it steps down in size and simplifies in design — through decades — until slide 8 shows a small 1950s round-shouldered fridge filled in fond Green with its door closed. A tiny decade label sits under it each slide ("2026" → "1950").

### Slide 1 — HOOK — DARK BG `#14161A`
> Your grandparents wasted almost no food.
> You waste $2,913 a year.
> What changed?

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 80pt, Off-White. "$2,913" in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red `#D64541`.
- **Visual:** Split composition. Left: 1950s kitchen silhouette — small fridge, bare counter, orderly. Right: modern kitchen silhouette — massive fridge, overflowing counters. Flat 2px line work, Off-White on Slate. The contrast answers the question before slide 2.
- **Source (20pt, Ink Gray):** EPA, April 2025 — household of 4.
- **Thread:** Modern mega-fridge outline, bottom-right, labeled "2026."

### Slide 2 — BACKUP HOOK — DARK BG `#14161A`
> They were poorer than you.
> They threw away less than you.
> That should bother you.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 72pt, Off-White, three stacked lines. Works standalone for the re-serve window — no dependence on slide 1.
- **Visual:** A single thin wallet icon beside a full dinner plate icon, both in Off-White outline with a fond Green check; opposite them, a fat wallet beside a trash can with a Signal Red cross. Wealth up, discipline down.
- **Thread:** Fridge one step smaller — "2010."

### Slide 3 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> They shopped daily. Bought what that day's meals needed. Leftovers became tomorrow's lunch — not a virtue, a necessity. The fridge was small because it didn't need to be large.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black.
- **Visual:** A small daily shopping basket (flat illustration, 5–6 item silhouettes) beside a one-day meal card: "breakfast · lunch · dinner." Basket and card drawn the same width — the visual equation: buy for today = eat everything = waste nothing.
- **Thread:** Fridge smaller — "1995."

### Slide 4 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> Three things changed. Fridges tripled in size (9 cu ft in the 1950s → 22 today). Weekly hauls replaced daily trips. And food got cheap enough to feel disposable.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black. Numbers in Space Grotesk Bold.
- **Visual:** Three stacked before/after rows, arrows left→right. Row 1: small fridge → huge fridge. Row 2: single bag → loaded cart. Row 3: "30% of income" → "10% of income" (both in Space Grotesk). Each row one accent color; the stack reads as a system change, not three trivia facts.
- **Source:** AHAM/DOE appliance data; USDA ERS Food Expenditure Series.
- **Thread:** Fridge smaller — "1980."

### Slide 5 — VALUE — DARK BG `#14161A`
> When food cost 30% of income, wasting it felt like burning money. At 10%, waste stops registering. A $4 bag of spinach doesn't feel like a loss. Multiply it by 52.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Off-White.
- **Visual:** Two pie charts side by side. Left: "1950s — food = 30% of income," large slice, Harvest Gold `#D9A441`. Right: "2026 — food = 10%," small slice, Steel Blue `#4A7BA6`. Beneath the right pie: "but $2,913/yr in waste" in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red `#D64541`. The small slice created the psychological permission.
- **Thread:** Fridge smaller — "1970."

### Slide 6 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> The irony: they were poorer and wasted less. You're richer and waste more. Abundance didn't solve the food problem. It made it invisible.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black. "invisible" in Steel Blue.
- **Visual:** A single minimal line graph. X-axis: "household income (adjusted)." Y-axis: "food waste per capita." One line rising to the right, in Signal Red `#D64541` (it charts loss). One small Ink Gray annotation: "the richer we got, the more we threw away."
- **Thread:** Fridge smaller — "1960."

### Slide 7 — THE REFRAME — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> Your grandparents didn't have a system.
> They had constraints. The constraints did the work.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 64pt, Slate Black for the reframe. Supporting row in DM Sans 32pt.
- **Visual:** The save-slide: three circled icons in a row — small fridge, daily calendar, thin wallet — captioned "forced visibility," "forced planning," "forced usage" in DM Sans 28pt. Constraints framed as features. Clean bordered card layout, built to be screenshotted.
- **Thread:** Fridge almost at final size — "1955."

### Slide 8 — CTA (fond card) — fond Green BG `#2E5E4E`
> You don't need her budget. You need her visibility.
> fond puts the constraints back — automatically.
> Send this to whoever does the groceries.
> Join the waitlist — link in bio ↓

- **Layout per standardized fond CTA card:** "fond" lowercase wordmark, DM Sans Medium, Off-White, centered. Reframe fragment in Playfair. Send-trigger at 70% opacity Off-White.
- **Thread:** The 1950s fridge sits complete at bottom-right, filled fond-Green-on-deeper-green tone, door closed, labeled "1950." The shrinking is finished — the constraint is restored.

## CAROUSEL #43 — "Your Fridge Is 5 Degrees Too Warm"

**Pillar:** 3 — Store It Right · **CTA Tier:** 2 · **Slides:** 8 · **Post day:** Friday
**THE ARC:** The assumption → the stakes → the science → the dial mystery → the proof → the 5-step fix → the payoff card → the close.
**CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A thermometer runs the full height of the right side rail on every slide. Slide 1: mercury at 40°F, column in Signal Red `#D64541`. Each slide the mercury drops fractionally and the color cools (red → amber `#D9A441` → blue). Slide 8: mercury rests at 37°F in Steel Blue `#4A7BA6` and the 37° tick mark becomes a fond Green `#2E5E4E` checkmark. Identical x-position every slide.

### Slide 1 — HOOK — DARK BG `#14161A`
> Your fridge is probably set to 40°F.
> It should be 37°F.
> Those 3 degrees cost you hundreds.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 80pt, Off-White. "40°F" in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red; "37°F" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue; "hundreds" in Signal Red.
- **Visual:** The thread thermometer itself is the hero this slide — enlarged, with a dotted target line at 37°F and a small label "$300–500/yr in spoilage" (Space Grotesk, Signal Red) bridging the tiny visual gap between the two temps. Tiny gap, large cost — that tension is the slide.
- **Thread:** Mercury at 40°F, Signal Red.

### Slide 2 — BACKUP HOOK — DARK BG `#14161A`
> Your milk isn't dying of old age.
> It's dying of heat.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 76pt, Off-White. "heat" in Signal Red `#D64541`.
- **Visual:** A milk carton icon with rising heat-wave lines above it (2px Off-White strokes). Standalone scroll-stopper for the re-serve window — works with zero context.
- **Thread:** Mercury drops a hair below 40°F.

### Slide 3 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> The FDA says "40°F or below." The "or below" is doing all the work. At 40°, bacteria slow down. At 37°, they slow far more. Every degree matters.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black. Temps in Space Grotesk Bold.
- **Visual:** A single curve graph. X-axis: 35–45°F. Y-axis: "bacterial growth rate." Curve rises steeply from 38° to 42°. Shaded zone 40–45° in Signal Red labeled "acceleration"; 35–38° zone in Steel Blue labeled "near-dormant." The exponential shape makes the argument in two seconds.
- **Source (20pt, Ink Gray):** FDA Food Code; USDA Food Safety guidelines.
- **Thread:** Mercury at ~39.5°F, red-amber.

### Slide 4 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> Most fridges show a 1–5 dial, not degrees. You have no idea what temperature you're at. The factory set it. You never touched it. Neither did the last owner.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Slate Black.
- **Visual:** Close-up flat illustration of a fridge dial pointing at "3," a large Playfair "?" beside it, captioned "3 = ???°F" in Space Grotesk. In the corner: a small fridge-thermometer icon with a price tag reading "$8" in fond Green `#2E5E4E` — the cheap fix, planted early.
- **Thread:** Mercury at ~39°F, amber `#D9A441`.

### Slide 5 — VALUE — DARK BG `#14161A`
> Milk at 40°F lasts 5–7 days. At 37°F: 7–10. Greens gain 2–3 days. Yogurt gains 4–5. One dial adjustment, made once, extends everything.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 36pt, Off-White. All figures Space Grotesk Bold.
- **Visual:** Three stacked comparison rows (milk carton, leafy greens, yogurt cup icons). Each row: left value in Off-White ("40°F → 5–7 days"), right value in Steel Blue ("37°F → 7–10 days"), a "+3 days" chip in fond Green `#2E5E4E` between them. Three examples prove the pattern is universal, not item-specific.
- **Thread:** Mercury at ~38.5°F, amber-blue transition.

### Slide 6 — VALUE — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> The fix: buy a fridge thermometer ($5–10). Middle shelf. Wait 12 hours. Read it. Adjust the dial. Check again tomorrow. Done forever.

- **Type:** DM Sans Regular 34pt, Slate Black. Prices/times in Space Grotesk.
- **Visual:** A five-step horizontal strip with numbered circles: 1 thermometer icon → 2 shelf placement → 3 clock ("12h") → 4 readout showing "37°F" in Steel Blue → 5 fond Green checkmark. Three words max per step. Dead simple by design.
- **Thread:** Mercury at ~38°F, cooling blue.

### Slide 7 — THE REFRAME — LIGHT BG `#F5F2EC`
> The cheapest way to extend every single item in your fridge:
> turn the dial down.

- **Type:** Playfair Display Bold, 64pt, Slate Black. "turn the dial down" in Steel Blue `#4A7BA6`.
- **Visual:** The save-slide is a mini reference card, bordered, screenshot-ready: "37°F — the number to hit" in Space Grotesk Bold hero size, with the three shelf-life gains from slide 5 restated in one compact row beneath. This is the slide people screenshot and act on tonight.

## SECTION 6 — DESIGN PRINCIPLES FOR THIS BATCH

1. One idea per slide. Never two.
2. Every swipe must feel like arrival somewhere new — no repeated layouts on consecutive slides.
3. Slide 2 is a second hook, not a continuation. It must stop a cold viewer who never saw slide 1.
4. Numbers are always Space Grotesk Bold. Signal Red `#D64541` marks loss and cost only — never decoration.
5. The thread never overlaps text and never exceeds 20% of slide area.
6. Slide 7 is the screenshot slide — design it as a bordered, saveable card.
7. Slide 8 is always the standardized fond CTA card on fond Green `#2E5E4E`, with a send/save-trigger line.
8. Copy is verbatim — do not rewrite, do not paraphrase, do not "improve."
9. The product is **fond**, lowercase, and appears only on slide 8.
