## SECTION 1 — BRAND SYSTEM (Recap)

This is the established visual system for the faceless account. Non-negotiable on every slide.

### Color palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Slate Black | `#14161A` | Dark backgrounds. Slide 1 and final slide always. |
| Off-White | `#F4F1E9` | Light backgrounds; primary text on dark. |
| Steel Blue | `#3D6E8F` | Brand/positive accent. Savings, the fond line, "what you should look at." |
| Signal Red | `#D72638` | **LOSS AND WASTE ONLY.** Never decorative. Hidden costs, expired food, the bad number. |
| Warm Amber | `#E0A33E` | Secondary highlight — comfort/manufactured-warmth cues, mid-stage warnings. |
| Confirm Green | `#2F9E44` | Checkmarks, "do this," verified-correct items. |

### Typography

| Font | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Space Grotesk | Bold | All numbers, stats, dollar amounts, math. Hero figures. |
| Playfair Display | Bold | Reframe lines and closing-argument copy on final slides. |
| DM Sans | Regular / Medium | Body copy, labels, captions, CTA line, the **fond** wordmark. |

### Layout rules

- 1080 × 1350 px (4:5 portrait).
- Safe margin 80 px all sides; nothing critical in outer 64 px.
- Body copy never exceeds **40 words** per slide.
- Numbers are always Space Grotesk Bold — never the body font.
- Signal Red is reserved for loss/waste figures only. Steel Blue for savings/positive.
- Dark/light backgrounds alternate every 2–3 slides. **Slide 1 dark. Final slide dark.**
- Flat illustration and diagrams only. No stock photography of people, no lifestyle imagery, no gradients, no drop shadows.
- Handle `@fond` bottom-center in DM Sans 24px, Off-White at 40% opacity, on every slide.

## SECTION 2 — CONTINUOUS VISUAL THREAD SYSTEM

Every carousel carries one design element that **progresses in a single direction across all slides and completes on the final slide**, timed to land with the CTA/reframe. It occupies a fixed position, stays under ~18% of slide area, and never competes with the text. This is what drives swipe-through.

The three threads in this batch are deliberately different **in kind** — a transforming object, a clearing haze, and a growing collection — so the batch never feels repetitive, and none repeats a thread used in the #31–33 batch (receipt tape / filling bar / reorganizing fridge).

### Thread summary — this batch

| # | Carousel | Thread element | Start state | End state | Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 36 | The Leftover Graveyard | A "graveyard row" that adds one container-headstone per slide | Empty ground line, one faint headstone | Full row of container-headstones under a red total | Bottom horizontal strip |

### CAROUSEL #34 — "The Sunday Fridge Audit"

- **Pillar:** 4 — Waste Hacks
- **CTA Tier:** Tier 2
- **Slides:** 6 · **Post day:** Sunday, Week 12
- **THE ARC:** The habit → the method → the sort → the rescue → the result → the reframe
- **CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A small fridge icon anchored top-right corner of every slide. Slide 1 it is cluttered and overstuffed (jumbled shapes bursting). Slide 2 it is empty (the audit begins). Slides 3–4 it fills back with color-coded, dated items. Slide 5 it is fully sorted with a small Confirm Green check appearing. Slide 6 the check is complete and the fridge glows faint Steel Blue. Position: top-right, ~14% of canvas. On dark slides the fridge is an Off-White line icon; on light slides a Slate Black line icon.

**Slide 1 — DARK BG (`#14161A`)**
> Every Sunday, open your fridge.
> Pull everything out.
> You'll be surprised what you find.

- *Type:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White, three stacked lines, centered-left.
- *Visual:* A wide-open fridge on the left, standing completely empty and clean; to its right, a counter overflowing with jumbled illustrated food shapes — jars, bags, containers, wilted greens — spread in disarray. Pure flat-illustration white/Off-White outlines on Slate Black. The contrast between the bare fridge and the chaotic counter is the hook: *is that what's in MY fridge right now?*
- *Thread:* Fridge icon (top-right) shown cluttered — overstuffed with jumbled shapes bursting past the door line.

**Slide 2 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> This takes 10 minutes. Group everything into three piles:
> **Use today** — expires within 48 hours.
> **Use this week** — good but aging.
> **Freeze now** — save what you won't reach in time.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. The three pile labels in DM Sans Medium, each in its zone color (Signal Red / Warm Amber / Steel Blue).
- *Visual:* Three labeled zones on a counter, color-coded like an ER triage sort. Left zone `#D72638`, "USE TODAY" (spinach, yogurt, cooked chicken). Middle zone `#E0A33E`, "THIS WEEK" (cheese, eggs, carrots). Right zone `#3D6E8F`, "FREEZE" (bread, extra meat, cooked rice). Flat item icons inside each zone; the triage layout itself carries the meaning.
- *Thread:* Fridge icon now **empty** — clean interior, door open, nothing inside. The audit has begun.

**Slide 3 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> The "Use Today" pile becomes tonight's meal. Don't plan around a recipe — plan around what needs to be eaten. Stir fry, sheet pan, soup, omelette. Any technique works.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. "what needs to be eaten" in Steel Blue Medium.
- *Visual:* The "Use Today" items from Slide 2, now tipped into a single pan on a stovetop — deliberately un-styled, not Instagram food. Just real food being used before it dies. Flat illustration, no steam-glamour, no garnish. On-brand: this is a rescue mission, not a food blog.
- *Thread:* Fridge icon begins **re-filling** — the Signal Red "use today" items have left; a few Warm Amber and Steel Blue dated items sit neatly on shelves.

**Slide 4 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> The "Freeze Now" pile takes 5 minutes. Bag it, label it, lay it flat. You just added 3–6 months of life to food that had 2 days left.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. "3–6 months" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue.
- *Visual:* A labeled freezer bag being laid flat into a freezer drawer, a date written on it in marker. Beneath, a small before/after timeline bar: "Before: 2 days" (Signal Red, short) → "After: 6 months" (Steel Blue, long). The time extension is the miracle; the bar makes it concrete.
- *Thread:* Fridge icon nearly full and orderly — items now on labeled shelves, spacing even, a small snowflake tag on the freezer section.

**Slide 5 — DARK BG (`#14161A`) — the number**
> Households that do a weekly fridge audit waste 40% less food. That's $1,165 saved per year. For 10 minutes on Sunday.

- *Type:* "40%" and "$1,165" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue (savings, positive framing), hero-sized, stacked. "10 min/week" beneath in smaller DM Sans Off-White.
- *Visual:* Vertical stack read top-to-bottom: big "40% less waste" → big "$1,165 / year" → small "10 min/week." The effort-to-result ratio is the argument; the small time figure at the bottom lands the punchline. Slate Black background, Steel Blue numbers read like a win, not a warning.
- *Thread:* Fridge icon fully sorted; a small Confirm Green `#2F9E44` check appears in the corner of the icon.
- *Source:* Weekly fridge-audit waste-reduction estimate; EPA household food-waste figure ($2,913/household/yr).

**Slide 6 — DARK BG (`#14161A`) — FINAL · CTA Tier 2**
> The best kitchen habit isn't a recipe.
> It's seeing what you have before you decide what to buy.
> fond does the audit for you — every day. Link in bio.

- *Type:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White for the reframe; "seeing" in Steel Blue. The **fond** CTA line in DM Sans Medium, Steel Blue `#3D6E8F`.
- *Visual:* Closing-card simplicity — pure type, generous whitespace, documentary end-card. The carousel that opened on an emptied fridge closes on the idea that *seeing* is the fix.
- *Thread:* **Completes.** Fridge icon fully sorted, Confirm Green check solid, and a faint Steel Blue glow around it — the ordered end-state of the whole journey.

### CAROUSEL #35 — "The Bakery Smell Is Fake"

- **Pillar:** 1 — Supermarket Secrets
- **CTA Tier:** Tier 2
- **Slides:** 6 · **Post day:** Monday, Week 13
- **THE ARC:** Comfort → suspicion → the science → the business case → the pattern → the reframe
- **CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A **warm-amber "scent haze"** occupying a full-width band along the top edge (~12% height) of every slide. Slide 1 the band is a thick amber fog, soft and inviting, partially obscuring the top edge. Each slide the haze thins and pulls back — by slide 5 it is a faint wisp, by slide 6 it is **gone**, leaving a crisp, clear edge. The thread physically enacts the arc: the manufactured warmth clears and you finally see through the manipulation. Amber `#E0A33E` at decreasing opacity (~70% → 55% → 40% → 25% → 10% → 0%).

**Slide 1 — DARK BG (`#14161A`)**
> That fresh bread smell when you walk in?
> It's pumped through the vents.

- *Type:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White, two stacked lines, centered-left.
- *Visual:* A minimal illustration of an HVAC vent / air duct with wavy aroma lines curling out of it, in Warm Amber `#E0A33E` — something that reads as cozy until the caption reframes it as industrial. The contradiction between "inviting smell" and "metal duct" is the hook. Stark on Slate Black.
- *Thread:* Scent-haze band at top is thickest here — a soft amber fog (~70% opacity) blurring the top edge, matching the aroma lines below it.

**Slide 2 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> Many grocery chains pipe bakery scent through HVAC systems near the entrance. It's called "scent marketing." The smell triggers hunger before you've picked up a basket.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. "scent marketing" in Steel Blue Medium.
- *Visual:* A clinical, blueprint-style floor plan of a store entrance. An arrow traces the path from an HVAC unit to the front doors; a "scent zone" is shaded Warm Amber over the first ~20 feet of the store. Treated like the engineered environment it is — architectural line-work, not lifestyle.
- *Thread:* Haze band thinner (~55% opacity) — the fog is starting to lift.

**Slide 3 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> A 2023 retail-psychology study found artificial bakery scent increased time in-store by 16% and basket size by 12%. The smell doesn't make you hungry. It makes you slow down.

- *Type:* "16%" and "12%" hero numbers in Space Grotesk Bold — 16% in Steel Blue (time), 12% in Signal Red (money spent). Body in DM Sans, Slate Black.
- *Visual:* The two hero numbers side by side, each with a small flat icon: a clock beside 16%, a shopping cart beside 12%. The pairing makes cause→effect undeniable. Clean, data-forward.
- *Thread:* Haze band (~40% opacity) — noticeably clearing.
- *Source:* 2023 retail scent-marketing study (time-in-store +16%, basket +12%).

**Slide 4 — DARK BG (`#14161A`)**
> It's not just bakeries. That $5.99 rotisserie chicken is a loss leader — the store loses money on it. But the smell pulls you into the hot-food section where margins run 60%+.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Off-White. "$5.99" and "60%+" in Space Grotesk Bold — $5.99 in Steel Blue (the bait), 60%+ in Signal Red (the hook margin).
- *Visual:* A simple two-node diagram. Left node: rotisserie chicken icon, "$5.99 — loss" (Steel Blue). Arrow → right node: deli counter icon, "sides, salads, prepared meals — 60% margin" (Signal Red). The arrow *is* the story: chicken is bait, prepared food is the hook.
- *Thread:* Haze band nearly gone (~25% opacity) — a thin amber wisp at the very top.

**Slide 5 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> Fresh flowers at the entrance. Warm lighting in produce. Cool lighting on the meat to make beef look redder. Nothing in the store is accidental.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. One-word panel labels in DM Sans Medium.
- *Visual:* A triptych — three small panels in a row. Panel 1: flowers under a warm overlay, label "Calm." Panel 2: produce aisle in golden light, label "Fresh." Panel 3: meat case in cool blue-white light, label "Red." Each label sits below its panel. The viewer realizes every visual cue they've absorbed was designed.
- *Thread:* Haze band a faint trace (~10% opacity) — almost cleared.

**Slide 6 — DARK BG (`#14161A`) — FINAL · CTA Tier 2**
> You're not shopping. You're being directed.
> fond shows you what you actually need before you walk in. Link in bio.

- *Type:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White for the reframe. The **fond** line in DM Sans Medium, Steel Blue `#3D6E8F`.
- *Visual:* Two clean lines of type, generous whitespace. The simplicity after the visual density of slides 2–5 is the emotional exhale — the closing argument.
- *Thread:* **Completes.** Scent haze is **fully gone (0%)** — the top edge is crisp and clear. The manufactured warmth has lifted; you see the store for what it is.

### CAROUSEL #36 — "The Leftover Graveyard"

- **Pillar:** 2 — The $2,913 Problem
- **CTA Tier:** Tier 2
- **Slides:** 7 · **Post day:** Wednesday, Week 13
- **THE ARC:** Recognition → the timeline → the psychology → the math → the worst offenders → the fix → the reframe
- **CONTINUOUS THREAD:** A **"graveyard row"** along the bottom ~15% of every slide — a thin ground-line on which one small **container-shaped headstone** is added per slide. Slide 1: bare ground line, a single faint headstone. Slides 2–5: one more container-headstone appears each swipe, each a rounded-lid tub silhouette (a leftover container styled as a tombstone). Slide 6 the fix arrives and one headstone is pulled upright/rescued (turned Confirm Green). Slide 7: the full row sits beneath a Signal Red total — the graveyard the title promised, now complete. Position: bottom horizontal strip. Ground line is a faint Off-White rule on dark slides / faint Slate line on light slides; headstones in muted grey, the rescued one in `#2F9E44`.

**Slide 1 — DARK BG (`#14161A`)**
> There are leftovers in your fridge right now
> that you will never eat.
> You already know which ones.

- *Type:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White, three stacked lines, centered-left.
- *Visual:* A fridge shelf, deliberately **slightly blurred / out of focus.** Three containers are visible but you can't quite make out what's in them. The blur is the concept: it's how you stop *seeing* food that's sat too long — it becomes background noise. The viewer feels caught.
- *Thread:* Bare ground-line at bottom with one faint grey container-headstone standing on it.

**Slide 2 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> Day 1: "I'll eat this tomorrow." Day 2: "I'll have it for lunch." Day 3: it moves to the back. Day 5: you forget it exists. Day 7: it's gone.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. Day labels in DM Sans Medium; "it's gone" in Signal Red.
- *Visual:* A horizontal left-to-right timeline. Five small fridge-container icons, each one progressively more faded/translucent than the last; by Day 7 the container is nearly invisible. The visual decay mirrors the mental decay — food doesn't rot suddenly, it fades from awareness.
- *Thread:* Second container-headstone appears on the row (now two).

**Slide 3 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> This isn't about discipline. It's about design. Leftovers go to the back because new groceries load in front. The old food becomes invisible by default.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. "design" in Steel Blue Medium.
- *Visual:* A top-down fridge diagram. New items labeled in Steel Blue at the front of each shelf; old items dimmed/greyed at the back. Arrows show the flow — new pushes old deeper. The viewer sees the fridge's own layout creates the problem.
- *Thread:* Third container-headstone appears (now three).

**Slide 4 — DARK BG (`#14161A`) — the number**
> The average household throws away 6–8 leftover containers per week. At $3–5 per serving, that's $18–40 a week. Just in leftovers.

- *Type:* "6–8 containers" and "$18–40 / week" in Space Grotesk Bold; "$936–2,080 / year" in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red, largest of the three. Body/labels DM Sans, Off-White.
- *Visual:* A row of 6–8 small container icons lined up like evidence, then a zoom-out escalation stacked beneath: "$18–40 / week" (Signal Red) → "$936–2,080 / year" (Signal Red, larger). The escalation from containers → weekly → annual should feel like a zoom-out that keeps getting worse. Slate Black makes the red read as alarm.
- *Thread:* Fourth container-headstone appears (now four) — the row is visibly becoming a graveyard.
- *Source:* Household leftover-waste estimate; per-serving $3–5; EPA annual food-waste figure.

**Slide 5 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`)**
> The top 5 most-wasted leftovers: rice, pasta, soup, roasted vegetables, cooked chicken. All of them freeze perfectly. Almost nobody freezes them.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. "14%" in Space Grotesk Bold, Signal Red.
- *Visual:* Five icons in a row (rice bowl, pasta plate, soup pot, roasting pan, chicken leg); each with a small snowflake below and "freezes well" in Steel Blue. Beneath the whole row: "% of households that freeze leftovers: 14%" in Signal Red. The gap between "all freeze perfectly" and "almost nobody does it" is the entire slide.
- *Thread:* Fifth container-headstone appears (now five) — full row of tombstones.

**Slide 6 — LIGHT BG (`#F4F1E9`) — the fix**
> Restaurants use FIFO: First In, First Out. Oldest items to the front, newest to the back. It takes 30 seconds when you unload groceries. It saves hundreds a year.

- *Type:* DM Sans body, Slate Black. "First In, First Out" in Steel Blue Medium; "30 seconds" in Space Grotesk Bold, Steel Blue.
- *Visual:* A two-panel before/after. Left: "How you load your fridge" — new in front, old hidden behind. Right: "FIFO" — old pulled forward, new placed behind; rotation arrows and a Confirm Green check on the right panel. The fix is so easy it makes the problem feel absurd.
- *Thread:* One headstone in the row is **pulled upright and rescued** — turns Confirm Green `#2F9E44`. The fix reaches back into the graveyard.

**Slide 7 — DARK BG (`#14161A`) — FINAL · CTA Tier 2**
> Your fridge isn't failing you. You just can't see what's in it.
> fond tracks every item and alerts you before anything becomes a leftover graveyard. Link in bio.

- *Type:* Playfair Display Bold, Off-White for the reframe; "leftover graveyard" in Signal Red. The **fond** line in DM Sans Medium, Steel Blue `#3D6E8F`.
- *Visual:* Closing card — pure type, generous whitespace. The callback to the title ("leftover graveyard") closes the loop.
- *Thread:* **Completes.** The full row of container-headstones sits along the bottom under a small Signal Red total tag ("6–8 / week"), with the single rescued Confirm Green stone standing among them — the graveyard the title promised, plus the one you can still save.

## SECTION 6 — DESIGN PRINCIPLES (governing this batch)

1. **Slide 1 always contains a visual contradiction, mystery, or discomfort** — the empty-fridge/full-counter split (#34), the cozy-smell/industrial-duct clash (#35), the blurred "you already know which ones" shelf (#36). That tension is the swipe trigger.
2. **Interior slides use diagrams, timelines, triptychs, and spatial layouts** — not text on colored blocks. The visual should teach even on mute with captions off.
3. **One "evidence" slide per carousel** — the triage sort (#34 S2), the store blueprint (#35 S2), the container-evidence row (#36 S4). It makes the account read like journalism, not opinion.
4. **The dark-background slide is the emotional/number escalation point** — #34 S5 (the win), #35 S4 (the margin trap), #36 S4 (the annual figure).
5. **The final slide returns to simplicity** — pure type, whitespace, one closing argument. Restraint after density reads as a verdict.
6. **Color discipline:** Signal Red only on loss/waste/hidden-cost figures; Steel Blue on savings and the fond line; Warm Amber only on manufactured-comfort cues (#35's haze and warm-light panel); Confirm Green only on the "do this"/rescued items.
7. **Every visual is flat-illustratable** — no 3D, no photography, no people. Diagrams, icons, and type-driven layouts are the whole palette.
