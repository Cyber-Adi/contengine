Slide 1: The checkout lane is a trap. Here's how it works.
Slide 2: The hunger window. Most people grocery shop after work or before dinner, when they're hungry. Checkout candy, chips, and drinks are placed at eye level for adults and grab level for kids. This isn't convenience. It's behavioral targeting.
Slide 3: The wait tax. Long lines aren't always understaffing. Some retailers intentionally slow checkout to increase dwell time near impulse products. Self-checkout is faster, but notice how the candy is still there.
Slide 4: Magazine and gum placement. Low-margin, high-impulse items are placed where you stand still. You've already committed to the purchase mentally by being in line. Adding $3 feels like nothing against a $120 cart.
Slide 5: The "cold case" near checkout. Drinks at the register are marked up 40, 100% compared to the beverage aisle. Same brand. Same size. Different price. You're paying for proximity and thirst.
Slide 6: The small cart illusion. Some stores introduced smaller carts to seem eco-friendly. Others made carts larger over the last decade. Bigger cart = your haul looks smaller = you buy more to "fill" it.
Slide 7: Every aisle is a funnel. The store profits when you overbuy and overwaste. fond helps you track what you actually have, so you only buy what you need. Save it before your fridge does the forgetting. Follow for more, something's on the way.
