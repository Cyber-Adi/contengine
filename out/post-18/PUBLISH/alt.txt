Slide 1: "10 for $10" does not mean you have to buy 10.
Slide 2: Most "10 for $10" deals ring up at $1 each no matter how many you grab. The store formats it as a bulk number because your brain reads it as a bundle. You feel pressure to hit the number. That's the point.
Slide 3: Same trick, different label: "3 for $9" yogurts. Each one scans at $3. You don't need 3. You just think you do because the sign grouped them.
Slide 4: End-cap displays use the same psychology. The product isn't on sale, it's on a paid advertising spot. Brands pay thousands for end-cap placement. That "deal" is a media buy, not a discount.
Slide 5: BOGO (Buy One Get One) is the most effective version. You needed one. Now you have two. The second one expires before you use it. Net cost: higher than buying one at full price.
Slide 6: The rule: check the unit price tag on the shelf (small print, bottom left). It shows cost per ounce or per count. That number is the only number that matters.
Slide 7: Grocery stores are designed to make you overbuy. Every extra item you don't eat is money in the trash. fond tracks what you actually use, so you buy what you need. Save it before your fridge does the forgetting. Follow for more, something's on the way.
