Slide 1: 7 foods you're refrigerating that should be on the counter.
Slide 2: Tomatoes. Cold destroys the enzymes that create flavor. Counter: up to 7 days once ripe. Fridge turns them mealy and tasteless.
Slide 3: Bananas. Refrigerating unripe bananas halts ripening permanently. Leave them on the counter until yellow with spots. Only refrigerate once fully ripe, then you get 4, 5 more days (skin browns, flesh stays fine).
Slide 4: Potatoes. Cold converts their starch to sugar, making them gritty and overly sweet when cooked. Cool, dark pantry: 3, 5 weeks. Fridge: ruined texture, ruined flavor.
Slide 5: Onions. Whole onions need airflow. Fridge moisture softens and molds them faster. Pantry: 1, 3 months. Once cut, then refrigerate, and use within 7, 10 days.
Slide 6: Garlic. Refrigeration triggers sprouting and mold. A cool, dry spot with ventilation keeps whole bulbs fresh 3, 6 months. Individual cloves: 3 weeks.
Slide 7: Avocados (unripe). Counter until ripe, 3, 5 days. It yields to gentle pressure when ready. Then fridge for 2, 3 more days. Only refrigerate after ripe, never before.
