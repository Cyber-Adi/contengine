Slide 1: Buying in bulk saves you money. Unless you throw half of it away.
Slide 2: The unit price on a 5 lb bag of spinach is 40% lower than the 5 oz container. Sounds like a win. But the average person uses 30% of the bag before it goes bad. The actual cost per usable ounce is higher.
Slide 3: Costco's business model depends on this math. The membership fee is subsidized by the assumption that you'll overbuy perishables. Rotisserie chicken at $4.99 gets you in the door. The $14 bag of avocados you throw half of covers the margin.
Slide 4: The breakeven question:. Before buying bulk produce, ask: "Will I use 100% of this before it spoils?". If the answer is no, the smaller size is cheaper.
Slide 5: Bulk buying works for:. dry goods, frozen items, canned foods, cleaning supplies, anything with a shelf life measured in months, not days.
Slide 6: Bulk buying fails for:. leafy greens, berries, fresh herbs, bread, dairy, and any produce you "might" use. "Might" means "won't.".
