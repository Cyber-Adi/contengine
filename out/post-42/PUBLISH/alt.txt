Slide 1: Your grandparents wasted almost no food. You waste $2,913 a year. What changed?
Slide 2: They were poorer than you. They threw away less than you. That should bother you.
Slide 3: They shopped daily. Bought what that day's meals needed. Leftovers became tomorrow's lunch - not a virtue, a necessity. The fridge was small because it didn't need to be large.
Slide 4: Three things changed. Fridges tripled in size (9 cu ft in the 1950s -> 22 today). Weekly hauls replaced daily trips. And food got cheap enough to feel disposable.
Slide 5: When food cost 30% of income, wasting it felt like burning money. At 10%, waste stops registering. A $4 bag of spinach doesn't feel like a loss. Multiply it by 52. Bar chart comparing two values.
Slide 6: The irony: they were poorer and wasted less. You're richer and waste more. Abundance didn't solve the food problem. It made it invisible.
Slide 7: Your grandparents didn't have a system. They had constraints. The constraints did the work.
Slide 8: You don't need her budget. You need her visibility. fond puts the constraints back - automatically. Send this to whoever does the groceries.
