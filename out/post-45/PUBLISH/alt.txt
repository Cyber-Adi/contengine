Slide 1: That apple looks fresh because the store wants it to.
Slide 2: You have never once seen this store's food in its actual condition. Only its optimized one.
Slide 3: Produce misting systems spray a fine water mist every few minutes. It makes vegetables look dewy and fresh. It also adds water weight - you're paying per pound for water - and accelerates rot by introducing moisture to surfaces.
Slide 4: Apples in the produce section may be up to 12 months old. Controlled-atmosphere storage (low oxygen, low temperature) suspends ripening. A wax coating restores the shine.
Slide 5: Meat cases use specific lighting. Red-spectrum lights make beef look redder and fresher. Under neutral light, the same steak looks brown and dull. The meat hasn't changed. The light has.
Slide 6: Bakery items labeled "baked fresh today" may have arrived frozen and been reheated on-site. The legal definition of "baked" includes finishing a par-baked product. Fresh is a feeling, not a fact.
Slide 7: Misting, waxing, lighting, and labeling are all legal. None are disclosed at the point of sale. Freshness is a visual performance, not a guarantee.
