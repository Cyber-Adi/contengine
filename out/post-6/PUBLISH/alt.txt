Slide 1: The date on your food is not regulated by the federal government.
Slide 2: 44% of Americans think the government oversees food date labels. It doesn't. The only federally mandated date label in the U.S. is on infant formula. Everything else is the manufacturer's best guess at peak quality.
Slide 3: "Best by" = quality, not safety. "Sell by" = stock rotation for the store, not a warning for you. "Use by" = the closest thing to a safety date, and even that one confused 49% of people surveyed.
Slide 4: 43%. 43% of consumers say they always or usually throw food away based on the date label alone. Up from 37% in 2016. The confusion is getting worse, not better.
Slide 5: Gen Z and millennials are the worst offenders:. 52% and 51% always or usually toss food at the label date. Boomers? 32%.
Slide 6: Manufacturers benefit from you throwing food out early. Earlier disposal = faster repurchase cycle. The dates aren't designed to protect you. They're designed to sell more product.
Slide 7: The sniff test is more reliable than the printed date for most refrigerated foods. Your nose evolved over millions of years. The label was printed last Tuesday.
Slide 8: Stop trusting the sticker. Start trusting the food.
