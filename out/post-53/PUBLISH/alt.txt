Slide 1: You're probably refrigerating half these foods. Refrigerating them is making them worse. And you've been doing it your whole life.
Slide 2: The rule: Cold air preserves food vulnerable to bacteria and moisture loss. But cold also breaks down starches in vegetables, stops ripening mid-track, and turns fats grainy. Not every food benefits. The question is always: is this food vulnerable, or is it alive?
Slide 3: Tomatoes: Cold destroys the enzymes that create flavor. A refrigerated tomato tastes like a tomato-shaped nothing. Counter: 3-5 days ripe, full flavor. Fridge: 2 weeks, no flavor. Avocados: Cold stops ripening entirely. Counter until ripe, then fridge for 1-2 days max - any longer and the flesh browns and goes mushy.
Slide 4: Bananas: Fridge cold turns the skin black within hours. The fruit is fine, but it looks rotten - and confusion follows. Counter until your preferred ripeness, then freeze (not refrigerate) to pause. Onions & Garlic: Cold and humidity make them sprout and go mushy. They need airflow and dry conditions - a counter bowl or mesh bag. Exception: cut onion -> fridge, airtight container only.
Slide 5: Potatoes: Cold converts starch to sugar, giving them an unnaturally sweet taste and causing them to darken when cooked. Store in a cool, dark, dry place - pantry, never fridge. Never store with onions; they accelerate each other's spoilage. Bread: Refrigeration stales bread 3x faster through starch retrogradation. Counter for up to 4 days. Freeze beyond that - never refrigerate.
