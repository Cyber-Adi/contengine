Slide 1: 80% of Americans have thrown away food based on a date the government didn't set.
Slide 2: There are three different date labels. They mean three different things. None of them mean "unsafe after this date.". Best By - quality estimate. Made by the manufacturer. Sell By - stock rotation instruction. For the store, not you. Use By - the closest to a real deadline. Still usually conservative.
Slide 3: The US has no federal law requiring date labels on most foods. The FDA encouraged "Best If Used By" as a standard in 2017. The food industry ignored it. Ambiguity is profitable. When you throw food away early, you buy more sooner.
Slide 4: 20%. Date label confusion causes an estimated 20% of all household food waste in the US. On a $2,913 annual waste figure, that's $580/year thrown away because of a label that has no legal definition.
Slide 5: The actual test for whether food is safe has nothing to do with a label. Smell it. Off means off - your nose is reliable. Look at it. Mold, slime, or discoloration = toss. Texture check. Mushy where it should be firm = gone. If it passes all three: it's fine.
Slide 6: The most over-tossed foods in American kitchens - and how long they actually last after the label date:. Milk - 5-7 days past "Sell By". Hard cheese - 3-4 weeks past "Best By". Greek yogurt - 2-3 weeks past "Best By". Bread - 5-7 days past "Best By" (or freeze it). Dry pasta - 1-2 years past "Best By". Eggs - 3-5 weeks after purchase.
Slide 7: The label tells you when the manufacturer stops being responsible for quality. It says nothing about when the food stops being safe. fond tracks real shelf life - not label dates. Save this for your next grocery run. Follow for more, something's coming.
